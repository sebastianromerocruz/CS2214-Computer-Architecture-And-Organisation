<h2 align=center>Week VII</h2>

<h1 align=center>Midterm Review</h1>

---

The midterm is **Thursday, October 15, 2026**. This review is a practice midterm: a set of five questions, in the same style as the real exam, that we first attempt on our own and then go through together.

**The questions are in this PDF: [Practice Questions](assets/Practice%20Questions_v1.pdf).** Read it on your screen, and write your answers on paper or on your iPad.

## What you can use

On the real midterm you will have these two handouts, so use them here too:

- the [E15 Cheat Sheet](assets/handout-e15-cheatsheet.pdf)
- [Section 3 of the E20 Manual](assets/handout-e20-manual-section3.pdf) (the instruction set)

They are there so you don't need to memorise instruction encodings. Nothing else is allowed: no notes, no phone, no calculator, no simulator.

## What's on it

| Question | Topic |
|---|---|
| 1 | Circuits and Verilog: write a combinational module for a circuit, then fill in its truth table |
| 2 | Bits and two's complement: a one-line C bit trick, and addition with overflow |
| 3 | The E15: where does the program counter go next, and what does a machine-code word do |
| 4 | Reading E20 programs: trace a loop, and follow a `lw` and some bitwise instructions |
| 5 | Writing an E20 program: a loop over two arrays |

## How to use this review

1. **Attempt first.** Try every question before you look at any answers. A wrong attempt teaches you more than reading a correct one, because it shows you exactly where your method breaks.
2. **Then go through the solutions.** There is an animated [slideshow](slides/solutions-lecture-animation.html) that walks through every answer (use the arrow keys), and the written [solutions](solutions.md), which give the answer and then the reasoning behind it, so you can compare *how* you got there and not only what you got. Look at the reasoning first: a right answer reached by a shaky method is worth checking as much as a wrong one.
